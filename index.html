Abstract

Carbon regulation is no longer merely an environmental concern; it is a core macroeconomic determinant of whether emerging economies converge with, or diverge from, the advanced world. The transition to a low-carbon economy represents the most profound structural shift in the international economic order since the Industrial Revolution, arriving precisely when the historic linkage between fossil-fuel consumption and catch-up growth has been broken.

This report, authored under the H Heuristics brand, investigates how global climate policy is reshaping the development trajectory of emerging markets. It augments classical and endogenous growth models with carbon constraints, analyzes the mechanics of climate policy instruments and carbon pricing, and quantifies the trade exposure created by the European Union’s Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM). It further examines technology transfer and leapfrogging, sectoral impacts across energy, manufacturing, agriculture, and transport, the financing of the transition through climate finance and sovereign debt, and the geopolitics of critical minerals.

Drawing on empirical data spanning energy investment flows, carbon tariff exposure, and sovereign debt pricing, the analysis finds that passive compliance risks stunted convergence, stranded assets, and climate-aggravated debt crises, while proactive green industrial policy offers emerging economies a credible path to leapfrog toward a sustainable, multipolar era of shared prosperity.

Keywords: carbon regulation, economic convergence, emerging economies, carbon pricing, carbon border adjustment mechanism (CBAM), green industrial policy, climate finance, technology transfer, stranded assets, energy transition.
